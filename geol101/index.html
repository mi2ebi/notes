01.2 origin of the solar system

big giant nebula that starts spinning around a bunch ~4.6 bya

nebulae don't just spontaneously start collapsing, they have enough internal pressure to avoid that. so something like a nearby supernova must have caused it. but i guess this isn't an astronomy class

01.3 early history of the earth

earth forms by accretion of some of the debris in our part of the nebula. asteroid impacts create a lot of heat, but not a ton

then around 4.5 bya, a planet the size of mars hits us! it melts the entire planet and some of the impact creates the moon. because earth is a completely molten ball now, the denser molecules sink to the center (planetary differentiation; this also happened to the other terrestrial planets).

layers

some layers are defined by composition:

crust
rich in aluminum and silicon
mantle
rich in iron, magnesium, and silicon. thickest
outer core
mostly iron and nickel, liquid
inner core
mostly iron, solid

some are defined by stiffness:

lithosphere
rigid, (relatively) cold rocks. crust + outer part of mantle
asthenosphere
soft warm rock. under the lithosphere

01.4 plate tectonics (basics)

analogy with skulls

geologic activity occurs along plate boundaries. there are 7 major plates (area > 20 million km²), 15 minor plates (area > 1 million km²), and "lots" of micro plates (area < 1 million km²). the specific number of plates is a point of contention

the movement of plates is driven by heat transfer / convection inside earth. hot parts of the mantle rise up and cold parts sink. very slowly, it takes millions of years. the convection moves the plates around

types of plate boundaries:

divergent boundary
new lithosphere is being created by rising mantle. plates move away from each other
convergent boundary
lithosphere gets destroyed or folded
transform boundary
plates slide against each other

02 minerals

02.1 chemistry review

isotope = version of an element with a different number of neutrons

ion is similar (different number of electrons)

avg atomic mass is a weighted average of all its isotopes

an ionic bond is when atoms stick together so they're both happy (full outer shells). eg salt = halite = Na−Cl+. ionic bonds aren't v strong and are soluble in polar liquids

a colavent bond is when atoms share electrons. these bonds are strong and insoluble. might end up with a polar molecule where it's electrically neutral overall but has a positive side and a ngeative side

a mixed bond is part ionic and part covalent. eg calcite: Ca2+CO32−

02.2 minerals

naturally occurring, formed by geologic processes, solid, crystalline, welldefined composition

formation

igneous
mid- lava/magma cooling
weathering
decomposing at earth's surface
precipitation
dissolved stuff in water form a solid after the water is gone
metamorphosis
high heat + pressure
diagenesis
low heat + pressure, usually with water

crystalline structure:

crystal lattice
building block of crystals. actual crystals may be any shape
crystal habit
the shape that forms under favorable conditions

quartz (SiO2) is a crystal as a mineral (most sand is quartz), but it's also the main ingredient in glass, which is amorphous, because for glass you melt sand and then cool it really fast

minerals are defined by their crystal structure, so similar atoms can be substitued and it's the same mineral. eg Mg is similar to Ca in size and electrons, and usually less than 4% of calcite has magnesium instead of calcium

02.4 mineral classes

based on the anion (negative charge)

there are >4000 minerals but only like 30 are common

class definition examples
silicates contain silicon and oxygen. most of earth's crust is these augite, biotite, chlorite, hornblende, muscovite, olivine, orthoclase, plagioclase, quartz, talc, garnet
carbonates contain CO32− (also nitrates are in this group) calcite (CaCO3), dolomite
sulfates contain SO42− gypsum, barite
halides contain a halogen ion eg Cl− or F− halite (NaCl), flourite
oxides and hydroxides oxygen or OH− combined with another element apatite, bauxite, corundum, hematite, limonite, magnetite
sulfides S2− + a metal galena (PbS)
native elements just 1 element by itself graphite, copper

all silicates contain the silica tetrahedron (SiO4)4− which can be combined in various ways

02.5 identifying minerals

one option: send your sample to a company that will glue a really thin shaving of it to a microscope slide (takes 6-8 weeks) and then put it under a $15k petrographic microscope that shines polarized light through it

slightly better option: put 1 crystal in an xray diffraction machine, spray liquid nitrogen on it for 8 hours as the xray spins around and takes pictures. computer fins the distance between the atoms. the machine costs ~$250k and needs a special room and operator. running a sample costs $800 to $1k

in the real world:

  1. assume it's one of the common minerals
  2. use the phsyical properties

most rocks have a specific gravity around 2.6

special properties

  1. magnetism
  2. smell (eg sulfur is awful)
  3. taste
  4. reaction to HCl (eg calcite, dolomite powder)
  5. fibrous texture (eg asbetos)
  6. bright color
  7. fluorescence (color under ultraviolet)
