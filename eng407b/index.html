intro to technical writing

technical writing refers to both the process and the product

per the society for technical communications, technical communication is any form of communication that is about technical/specialized topics, uses technology e.g. webpages or help files, or instructs on how to do something

technical writing also has a certain purpose: help others learn/do/decide something; reinforce or change attitudes; motivate people to take action

characteristics:

  • focused on a specific audience
  • helps said audience solve problems
  • professional
  • research oriented (and as a side effect, generally produced collaboratively)
  • designed for usability, a11y, etc
  • ethical

good technical writing is complete, concise, accurate, and clear

all the technical part means is it's about knowledge that isn't widespread. the challenge is, how do you explain it in a way a nonspecialist could understand?

windowpane theory of language
the notion that language provides a view of reality that may be either clear or obfuscated

problem: turns out, form and content can't be separated

technical writing used to not be taught very well. when that↑ was written, the situation was:

positivism
"who cares about rhetoric"
  1. no one knows what technical writing is! what topics count as technical? how should the writing quality be evaluated? "clarity" fails to distinguish low quality technical writing from non technical writing
  2. students aren't good at clarity anyway. if all that matters in technical writing is form, there isn't much to teach in a technical writing class – it ought to just be the student writing down facts they know. but it can't be that simple or else everyone would be good at it!
  3. scientists are taught to distance themselves from their subject matter, so they use things like passive voice often to achieve that. but the technical writing professor thinks that's not very elegant of them. what's the scientist supposed to do then?
  4. it's assumed anyone can understand the topic if presented with enough facts. so all the realizations of 'audience analysis' tend to just be changes in vocabulary, which aren't that helpful. maybe the topic needs to be explained in more pedagogical methods
